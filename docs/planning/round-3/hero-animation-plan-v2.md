# Hero animation: review and path forward

## 1. Review of v0 (senior animator)

v0 blends the 29 key frames point by point, at 24 frames a second, with the characters on twos and the pencil redrawn on every drawing.

| Question | Verdict | Evidence from the filmstrip |
|---|---|---|
| Does it feel hand-drawn? | **Not yet.** | The lines are thin, even and very regular. The jitter is random per point, so it reads as "fuzzy" rather than "drawn". The bodies are built from matched pairs of parallel outlines, which looks engineered. |
| Does it feel organic? | **No.** | Point blending moves every point in a straight line. Rotations shrink bodies (Tony swimming up, frames 98–104). When Juhu turns round she collapses into a vertical line (frames 206–210 and 222–226). Juhu glides through her leap in one frozen pose, like a paper cut-out. |
| Enough frames? | **The count is fine, the content is not.** | 240 frames are drawn, but each one is a straight blend between two key poses about 8 frames apart. Nothing happens between the poses: no breakdowns, no overlap, no leg or tail action. |
| Do the scenes flow into each other? | **Partly.** | These work: ground into water, the ball's bounces, the cycle shrinking into the sun, and the loop seam. These break: lines sweep in from off-screen (the flat road into the S-curve, frames 152–162); the rider "grows" as a blob inside the wheel (frame 144); the jump from the surface to under water is a pop. |
| Does it match johnmartin.in? | **The story and spirit, yes. The line and motion, no.** | His work uses a few confident strokes with pressure, a solid head, coherent wobble, and every drawing changes. Ours has many even strokes and stiff blended motion. |

**The root cause is one decision:** v0 blends drawings, when it should blend poses. Animation feels alive because joints rotate on arcs, limbs keep their length, and parts move at different times. A point-by-point blend can't do any of that, so adding more key frames alone won't fix it.

## 2. The path forward (animator + engineer)

**Principle: animate in pose space, draw in stroke space.**

### A. Engine v1: pose-space animation (engineer)
1. **Port the rig to JavaScript.** The animation then stores poses rather than points: root position, body pitch and joint angles for Tony and Juhu. The engine builds the strokes fresh for every frame, so limbs never shrink and joints always move on arcs.
2. **Blend each joint on its own curve,** with eases and small time offsets. Arms, tail and ears lag behind the body (overlapping action and follow-through).
3. **Pin feet and paws to the ground** while they touch it (IK), so there's no sliding.
4. **Procedural cycles:** the jog, Juhu's trot and gallop, the swim kick, pedalling and tail wag are generated by code. They give unlimited unique drawings with real leg action.
5. **Turning round:** Juhu turns with a three-pose turn, not a mirror blend, so she never collapses into a line.
6. **The camera becomes its own move** (pan, follow and zoom), instead of shifting every scene line.
7. **Small data:** poses are about 20 KB of JSON, against 888 KB of points today.

### B. Line quality v2 (animator + engineer)
1. **Pressure strokes:** each stroke is a ribbon that varies in width, thin at the start, fuller in the middle, with a light flick at the end.
2. **Coherent wobble:** smooth noise along the stroke, re-seeded 12 times a second (line boil), instead of random jitter on every point.
3. **Fewer, more confident strokes:** merge the paired outlines into gesture lines where the form allows. Use a slight overshoot at the corners, and the odd second pass.
4. **A dense pencil scribble for the head,** the pencil version of John Martin's solid head.
5. **A style board in the lab:** our frames side by side with John Martin's, to tune weight, wobble and stroke count.

### C. Acting and timing (animator)
1. **A dope sheet** (JSON): key poses, breakdowns, holds and eases for each beat, in frames.
2. **Breakdowns for the big actions:**
   - Juhu's leap: gather, extend, gather.
   - The push into the dive: anticipation, then the arc.
   - The swim-up roll.
   - The throw: wind-up, overshoot, follow-through.
   - Juhu's crouch, then launch.
3. **Smear drawings** (a stretched, blurred-looking frame) on the fastest moves: the dive and the throw.

### D. Transitions (both)
Each join gets a shared shape and a stroke-reveal ("trim path") instead of points flying in:

| Join | Plan |
|---|---|
| Ground into water | The same line; the wave grows. (Already good.) |
| Surface to under water | The camera follows Tony down. The surface line slides up out of frame. |
| Basket to road | The camera keeps following the ball down. The road enters from the bottom. |
| Ball into cycle | The tubes draw out from the hub one by one, then the rear wheel, then Tony. |
| Flat road into S-curve | The same line bends. The horizon draws outward from the vanishing point. |
| Sun into beach | The waves draw outward from the sun, then the shore, then the sand. |
| Beach into land | Horizon and waves trim back to the shoreline, which flattens into the ground. |

### E. Review tooling and checks
- **The lab:** scrubber, onion skin (ghosted neighbouring frames), John Martin reference side by side, curve view for each joint, and PNG-sequence export for Miro.
- **Automatic checks:**
  - no limb changes length by more than 2 %;
  - no character's width collapses below 30 % between frames;
  - the loop seam matches in position and speed;
  - steady 60 fps on a mid-range phone.

### F. Ship
- Choose p5.js (loaded last) or a plain-canvas port of the same code.
- Show a still drawing until it loads.
- Reduced motion shows a still drawing.
- A pause option (WCAG 2.2.2) is still to be decided.

## 3. Order of work

1. **Vertical slice: Act 1 only** (jog, Juhu leaps in, push, dive), built with A, B and C. This proves organic motion and the new line on 2 seconds before redoing everything. You review it in the lab.
2. **Acts 2–4 on the same system**, with the transitions (D).
3. **Polish, checks, and adding it to the site.**

The 29 key frames stay as the storyboard and staging reference. Their poses become the starting point for the pose data.
